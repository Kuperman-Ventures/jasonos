# Browning networking

Source documents: Networking 102, Working Your Browning Associates Networking Referrals, The 4 Topics, and The Ask and Why.

## Browning Networking - First Call

Description: Frequent Browning-introduced networking meetings with people of various backgrounds and relevance to my job search.

Situation: A first conversation with a Browning referral: a peer executive who spoke to Browning, agreed to connect, and has my resume, but does not know me and may not remember Browning.

Success sentence: They talk about themselves for most of the call, I give the 60-second snapshot once, and we leave with a dated second call on both calendars.

Checklist:

1. Opened with the attention getter: Browning, why them, purpose of the call
2. Paid one specific, genuine compliment from their resume
3. Asked whether they are still at their company and still looking before anything else
4. Asked how their search is going and what they are seeing
5. Offered to help them before asking for anything
6. Asked whether they have looked at my executive profile
7. Gave the snapshot in under 60 seconds
8. Did not ask directly for a job or whether their company is hiring
9. Made no more than one soft ask for contacts, or deferred the ask to call two
10. Set a date and time for the second call before hanging up
11. Said I would send a calendar invite and a thank-you note

Based on: Browning Associates method: Networking 102, Working Your Referrals, The 4 Topics, The Ask and Why. See docs/strategies/browning-networking.md.

### 1. Attention getter

Kind: agenda

### 2. Why them, why now

Kind: point

I got your resume from Browning. Reading it, [one specific thing from their background] stood out, and I thought we could be useful to each other. Here's what I was hoping to cover in the next 20 minutes.

### 3. One genuine compliment

Kind: point

I was impressed by [specific accomplishment]. I did something close to it at OUTFRONT / Omnicom and I'd like to hear how you approached it.

### 4. Assess and relate

Kind: agenda

### 5. Still at {{company}}? Still looking?

Kind: question

I'm not sure I have the latest version. Are you still at {{company}}? Browning mentioned you were looking at one point. Is that still the case?

### 6. How is the search going?

Kind: question

If looking: how long, what are you seeing, is it picking up, are you networking weekly. If landed: how did you find it, was it through networking, what are the challenges in the new role.

### 7. Offer help first

Kind: point

I have Browning's network and my own. Tell me who you're trying to reach and I'll see who I know.

### 8. Pick one of the 4 Topics

Kind: question

Industry: what's the biggest challenge in your industry right now. Company: are you aware of companies in transition, M&A, or looking for fractional leadership. Network: who in your network is the most connected. Resources: what tools or recruiters have helped you most.

### 9. Turn to me

Kind: agenda

### 10. Have you looked at my executive profile?

Kind: question

If no: I'd appreciate it when you get a chance. While I have you, may I give you a quick snapshot of where I am?

### 11. The 60-second snapshot

Kind: point

{{snapshot}} Target companies: {{targets}}.

### 12. Call to action

Kind: agenda

### 13. Can we circle back in two weeks?

Kind: ask

Prepare reactions: yes

I might have some good people for you, and your network may be able to help me. Can we circle back two weeks from today? I'll send a calendar invite. What day works?

- agree. They say: Sure, send me an invite. Pivot: Great. I'll send it for [day] at [time]. I'll have a couple of names for you by then.
- soft_agree. They say: Yeah, let's stay in touch. Pivot: Let's put something on the calendar so it actually happens. Does the week of the [date] work?
- defer. They say: I'm swamped the next few weeks. Pivot: Understood. I'll send a note in three weeks with a couple of times. Is email the best way to reach you?
- deflect. They say: You should really talk to my colleague about this. Pivot: I'd like that. Would you be willing to introduce us? And I'd still like to keep our line open.
- counter_ask. They say: What exactly are you hoping I can do? Pivot: Two things. Help me understand what's going on at {{targets}}, and let me help you with anyone you're trying to reach.
- decline. They say: I don't think I'm the right person to help. Pivot: That's fair. Can I at least stay connected on LinkedIn and send you anything useful I come across?

### 14. Who would you suggest I put on my radar?

Kind: ask

Prepare reactions: yes

Soft ask, only if the call is going well. Given your years in [industry], you're probably more plugged in than I am to who's doing what. Who would you at least suggest I put on my radar right now?

- agree. They say: A couple of people come to mind. Pivot: Who are they, and would you be comfortable with me mentioning your name?
- soft_agree. They say: Let me think about it and get back to you. Pivot: Thank you. I'll follow up in my thank-you note so it's easy to reply to.
- defer. They say: Let's talk again once I know you better. Pivot: That makes sense. That's exactly why I want the second call.
- deflect. They say: My network is mostly in [other industry]. Pivot: That's fine. We're not competing. Who in that industry knows people in mine?
- counter_ask. They say: What companies are you targeting? Pivot: {{targets}}. Who do you know that knows someone at any of those?
- decline. They say: I don't really know anyone who's hiring. Pivot: I'm not looking for people who are hiring. I'm looking for people worth knowing. Who's the most connected person in your network?

### 15. Close

Kind: point

I'm glad Browning connected us. I'm going to reach out to a few people where I think you'd be valued and hand them off when I hear back. Let's partner on this.

## Browning Networking - Second Call

Description: Follow-up call with a Browning referral after the first conversation and thank-you note.

Situation: A follow-up with a Browning referral after a first call and a thank-you note. They have my profile and we have a reason to talk again.

Success sentence: I deliver something useful to them, they name at least one specific person or company for me, and we agree on what happens next and when.

Checklist:

1. Opened with what I did for them since the first call
2. Confirmed they connected on LinkedIn and looked at my profile
3. Shared my target company list with geography
4. Asked for a specific list of companies they are targeting
5. Asked where they are seeing the most traction
6. Made one specific ask using a who question, not an if question
7. Did not accept "send me your resume" without asking for a conversation first
8. Agreed on the next step and date

Based on: Browning Associates method: Networking 102 (2nd Call section), The Ask and Why. See docs/strategies/browning-networking.md.

### 1. Open

Kind: agenda

### 2. What I did since we spoke

Kind: point

After our last call I reached out to [name] about [their need]. Here's where that stands.

### 3. Did you get a chance to look at my profile?

Kind: question

Any thoughts on how my background reads to someone in your seat?

### 4. Exchange

Kind: agenda

### 5. My target list

Kind: point

Here are the companies I'm focused on: {{targets}}. I'll put this in an email too.

### 6. Your target list

Kind: question

What companies are you trying to get into? Where are you seeing the most traction?

### 7. The ask

Kind: agenda

### 8. Who do you know at [target company]?

Kind: ask

Prepare reactions: yes

I noticed you're connected to [name] at [company]. That's a place I think I could add value. How well do you know them?

- agree. They say: I know them well. I'll make an intro. Pivot: Thank you. I'll send you two sentences you can forward so it's easy.
- soft_agree. They say: I can probably do that at some point. Pivot: Would it be too much to ask to send it this week? I'd like to reach them before [reason].
- defer. They say: Let me check with them first. Pivot: Of course. Would it help if I sent you a short note about why I want to talk to them?
- deflect. They say: I don't know them, but I know someone who does. Pivot: That's even better. Who do you know that knows someone there?
- counter_ask. They say: What would you want to talk to them about? Pivot: Not a job. I want to understand how [company] is thinking about [topic] and whether my background fits anywhere they're growing.
- decline. They say: I'd rather not make intros right now. Pivot: I understand. Is there anyone you'd be comfortable with me reaching out to on my own and mentioning your name?

### 9. If they offer to pass my resume along

Kind: ask

Prepare reactions: no

Thank you for offering. I have so many versions, let me give you my executive profile instead. When you talk to them, would it be possible to set up a short conversation first?

### 10. Close

Kind: agenda

### 11. Next step and date

Kind: point

Here's what I'll do by [date]. Can we check in again in three weeks?

## Jason's original points

These eight headlines were on "Browning Networking Meeting" (`4ac58e33-7f47-4ed0-b377-40051ff39c15`) before the seed replaced them. The line under each headline is the detail that was stored with it.

1. Open
   Thank {{name}} for the intro and confirm how much time we have. Set the frame: I want to make this useful for both of us, not just a favor call.
2. Give them the 60-second version of what I'm looking for
   Role type, industry focus, company size, and geography — enough that {{name}} can pattern-match to their network without me rambling. Prepare this cold before the call.
3. Cover the TLDR on whatever Browning sent over
   Review all materials Browning provided before the call. Pull out the 2-3 facts most relevant to {{name}}'s background so the conversation doesn't start from zero.
4. How did {{name}} get to where they are now?
   Their path from early career to current {{title}} at {{company}} — what moves mattered, what they'd do differently. Genuine context, not small talk. Useful for calibrating the ask later.
5. What's the job market looking like from where they sit?
   Ask what they're seeing in hiring, org changes, or budget at {{company}} or in their sector. They may surface something not visible from the outside.
6. Is there anyone else at {{company}} I should talk to?
   If {{company}} is a target, ask whether there's a hiring manager, team lead, or peer they'd suggest. Get a name, not a department.
7. Ask for intros to two specific people from their LinkedIn network
   Before the call, identify two people from {{name}}'s LinkedIn connections who fit my target criteria. Name both people specifically. Ask if {{name}} knows them well enough to send a warm note. Don't ask for a general intro to 'anyone who might help.'
8. Close and next step
   Thank {{name}} by name for the specific things they shared. Confirm any intros they agreed to and who sends the first message. Send a follow-up note within 24 hours.
